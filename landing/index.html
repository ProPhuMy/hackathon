Shared-fridge accountability

FRIDGECHECK

Who put it in. Who took it out. Whether it was theirs.

FridgeCheck watches a shared fridge with two ordinary phones: one recognizes the people, the other sees the food. When someone takes food that isn't theirs, it raises an alert within seconds, with evidence instead of guesses.

THE PROBLEM

Food disappears.

In dorms, offices and shared apartments, labels get ignored and nobody saw a thing. The only evidence is an empty shelf, and the only tool is a passive-aggressive sticky note.

WHY IT MATTERS

Money, waste, trust.

Missing food costs real money, ends up as waste, and quietly breaks trust between people who share a space. Without evidence, every conversation becomes an accusation.

THE SOLUTION

Facts, not guesses.

FridgeCheck records who put what in and who took what out, automatically. If you take someone else's food, it notices in seconds and shows it on a live dashboard.

HOW IT WORKS

Two cameras, two recognition services, one event stream, one judge.

  1. 1

    Phone 1 → Person service

    Faces the people at the fridge. Detects faces and recognizes them with ArcFace on the GPU in about 20 ms per frame. Strangers stay anonymous, and it would rather say nothing than say the wrong name.

  2. 2

    Phone 2 → Item service

    Sits on top of the fridge, watching the door. YOLOE spots hands and items; DINOv2 remembers each item, so the bottle that comes out is matched to the bottle that went in.

  3. 3

    Redpanda event stream

    Both services publish small, schema-checked events (person.seen, item.event) to a Kafka-compatible stream.

  4. 4

    Backend matcher

    A probabilistic matcher pairs every item event with the face seen at that moment (within about 1.5 s), then decides: ok, or wrong owner.

  5. 5

    Dashboard, alerts, AI

    A live dashboard shows the fridge, its inventory and alerts. An MCP server lets an AI assistant answer “who took my water?”.
